## Cost-Benefit Trade-offs of Metabolite Exchange in Obligately Dependent Cross-Feeding Microbial Pairs

### Thesis Project by Ayaan Dutt, BSc (Hons.) Physics, Ashoka University

Under the guidance of Prof. Sandeep Krishna (NCBS) and Prof. Gautam Menon (Ashoka University)

#### Abstract

Microbial communities frequently engage in metabolic cross-feeding, exchanging diffusible metabolites that can stabilize interactions and enhance fitness. Although a subset of cross-feeding pairs have been shown to outperform self-sufficient autonomous networks in efficiency, it remains unclear whether this advantage persists when the exchange is both obligate and biosynthetically costly. Building on the network-based framework of Goyal & Krishna (2022), we construct large ensembles of _in-silico_ metabolic networks derived from the KEGG reaction repertoire to investigate the conditions under which obligate cross-feeding can outperform autonomous metabolism. We assemble fully minimal autonomous networks capable of synthesizing essential biomass precursors from a minimal nutrient medium, and extend this construction to obligately dependent network pairs that exchange both byproduct and intermediate metabolites, including those which impose a direct fitness cost on the donor. Metabolic yields are estimated using a stoichiometry-based proportional flux-allocation scheme, and the resulting yield distributions are compared across autonomous and cross-feeding networks.

We find that, in contrast to the original results, the global outperformance effect disappears under the minimality constraint and modified flux allocation scheme: obligate cross-feeders fail to show a positive deviation from the autonomous yield distribution. Furthermore, yields appear largely agnostic to whether the exchanged metabolite is a byproduct or an intermediate. These findings indicate that the outperformance effect is sensitive to the degree of metabolic redundancy in the partner networks, and offer a network-based explanation for when and why costly obligate cross-feeding fails to confer a net fitness benefit.
